# Roadmap

The app's development is based on a curriculum. Each version of the app must meet the requirements for each level of that curriculum.

## The Complete Path

It is divided into **7 levels**:

| Level | Stage             | Question the person can answer                                 |
| ----- | ----------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0     | **Awareness**     | What is happening with my money? |
| 1     | **Control**       | Where is my money going? |
| 2     | **Organization**  | How should I allocate my money? |
| 3     | **Stability**     | What happens if something goes wrong? |
| 4     | **Growth**        | How can I grow my net worth? |
| 5     | **Optimization**  | How can I make my money work harder? |
| 6     | **Mastery**       | Can I maintain my financial well-being without excessive effort? |

But there is an important detail: **not everyone needs to complete the levels at the same pace**. Someone might be excellent at saving but have problematic debt. Another person might invest heavily but have no idea how much they spend.

That is why, within *Rumbo*, each level will focus on **competencies**, not just lessons.

***

# 0. Financial Awareness

### Objective

To help the person stop viewing money as something abstract.

A beginner typically thinks:

> “I don’t know where my money goes.”

The initial transformation is:

> “I can see what is happening.”

### Competencies

*   Understand what **income, expenses, savings, debt, and net worth** are.
*   Identify how much money is coming in.
*   Record transactions.
*   Distinguish between **needs, wants, and obligations**.
*   Recognize major money leaks.
*   Understand that spending less than one earns is the foundation of everything.

### Habits

At this stage, the goal isn't to create a perfect budget.

First:

**Record → Observe → Understand.**

For example:

> Record all expenses for 7 days. Not:

> “Create a detailed monthly budget with 17 categories.”

The first mission should be almost absurdly easy.

### 🏁 Mastery is achieved when...

The person can answer:

*   How much do I earn?
*   Approximately how much do I spend?
*   What do I spend the most on?
*   How much money do I have?
*   Do I have debt?

Without having to open five apps and conduct a session of financial archaeology.

***

# 1. 🎛️ Financial control

This is where the first major leap occurs.

The person is already **observing** their money. Now they learn to **control** it.

### Competencies

*   Creating expense categories.
*   Setting spending limits.
*   Creating a budget.
*   Distinguishing between fixed and variable expenses.
*   Planning for upcoming expenses.
*   Creating a system to review finances.
*   Avoiding consistently spending more than they earn.

### Fundamental concept

**Budget ≠ restriction.**

The budget should teach them:

> “This money has a purpose.”

Not:

> “You cannot spend.”

### Habits

For example:

**Every week → review expenses → compare against the budget → adjust.**

This is where one of the first recurring *Rumbo* habits appears.

### 🏁 Mastery

The person knows—**before spending**—how much they can spend.

That small change is huge.

***

# 2. 🧱 Financial organization

Once they control their monthly cash flow, they begin to build structure.

Here, I would introduce the concept of a **personal financial system**.

### Competencies

*   Organizing bank accounts.
*   Separating money by goals.
*   Automating transactions.
*   Creating funds for foreseeable expenses. *   Plan for annual expenses.
*   Set financial goals.
*   Save systematically.
*   Understand the concept of **savings rate**.

### A key point

Here, I would introduce the concept of **specific savings funds**:

*   car maintenance
*   vacations
*   gifts
*   insurance
*   taxes
*   repairs
*   major purchases

Because many people think:

> “I had a bad month because of an unexpected expense.”

When in reality, it was:

> “I had a perfectly predictable expense that I didn't plan for.”

That concept can be very powerful within the *Rumbo* framework.

### 🏁 Mastery

The individual begins to experience something new:

> **The future stops catching them off guard.**

***

# 3. 🛡️ Financial stability

This is where defenses come into play.

Before talking about aggressive investing, one must prevent a problem from destroying what has been built.

### Competencies

*   Understand financial risk.
*   Create an **emergency fund**.
*   Determine the required amount based on personal circumstances.
*   Distinguish between a real emergency and a discretionary expense.
*   Manage basic insurance coverage.
*   Create a plan for potential loss of income.
*   Understand the concept of good vs. bad debt without oversimplifying.
*   Reduce high-cost debt.

### Order of instruction

First:

**Small cushion → control dangerous debt → fully funded emergency fund.**

I wouldn't tell someone with credit card debt at 60% APR:

> “Start investing because the market historically yields 10%.”

That’s like putting a sail on a boat with a hole in it.

### 🏁 Mastery

A multi-month emergency **no longer automatically means going into debt**.

The person begins to build resilience.

***

# 4. 📈 Wealth growth

Now we can seriously talk about investing.

The person already controls their cash flow and has a reasonably stable foundation.

### Competencies

*   Understand what investing is.
*   Distinguish between saving and investing.
*   Understand risk vs. return.
*   Understand inflation.
*   Understand compound interest.
*   Know different asset classes.
*   Understand time horizon.
*   Understand liquidity.
*   Define investment goals.
*   Build an investment strategy.
*   Start investing.

### Here I would introduce

*   Government bonds (e.g., CETES) / fixed income
*   Funds
*   ETFs
*   Stocks
*   Bonds
*   Real estate
*   Retirement instruments

But **I wouldn't start by teaching products**.

First:

> What are you investing for?

Then:

> When will you need the money?

Then:

> How much risk can you tolerate?

And finally:

> Which instrument makes sense? ### 🏁 Mastery

The person can explain:

> “I am investing X amount, with this objective, this time horizon, and this risk level.”

Not:

> “My cousin told me to buy NVIDIA.”

***

# 5. ⚙️ Financial optimization

Here, we are no longer simply trying to “avoid making mistakes.”

We are looking to **make the system more efficient**.

### Competencies

*   Tax optimization.
*   Debt optimization.
*   Comparing financial products.
*   Reducing fees.
*   Optimizing accounts and cards.
*   Maximizing benefits without unnecessarily increasing consumption.
*   Optimizing savings and investments.
*   Reviewing asset allocation.
*   Planning major purchases.
*   Retirement planning.
*   Analyzing net worth.

### Example

One person might have:

> $100,000 in savings.

But another might have:

> $100,000 correctly distributed across liquidity, emergency funds, specific goals, and investments.

Both have the same amount of money.

**They do not have the same financial system.**

***

# 6. 🧠 Financial mastery

This is the level I find most interesting for Rumbo.

Because **being an expert doesn't mean being a millionaire**.

A person can earn $20,000 MXN a month and possess excellent financial mastery.

Another can earn $150,000 and live financially on the edge of a precipice.

### A person with financial mastery:

*   Knows their financial situation.
*   Controls their expenses.
*   Saves automatically.
*   Has reserves.
*   Manages their debt.
*   Invests in line with their goals. *   Has protection against risks.
*   Plans for the future.
*   Periodically reviews your finances.
*   Can make financial decisions without constant anxiety.
*   Knows when **not to do anything**.
*   Can adapt your strategy when life changes.

And here is something very important:

## The ultimate goal is not to optimize every single penny.

It is **buying freedom**.

You reach a point where your finances require maintenance, not constant monitoring.

# Project organization

Inside the `features` folder, there is a folder for each level. Within those folders, there are further specifications regarding the requirements and design of each level. There are also subfolders containing the plans for each feature.

> Each new feature is created as `spec/features/level-folder/NNN-name/` with `spec.md`, `plan.md`, and `tasks.md` before any code is touched.
